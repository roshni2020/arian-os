Entry 3 10:24:13 48.7% +0.4 pts KEEP · FROZEN

max_depth 4 → 3 on the full-input XGBoost

ObservedExp-002 improved validation AUPRC from 0.3972 to 0.4830 and cut false negatives from 262 to 235, but train AUPRC 0.7269 versus validation 0.4830 leaves a 0.2439 gap. The model stopped at iteration 459 of 1000, so estimator capacity is not the limiting factor; the remaining gap motivates lower tree complexity.

HypothesisKeeping the full feature protocol, five-day weather window and all other XGBoost settings fixed while reducing max_depth from 4 to 3 will reduce overfitting and raise validation AUPRC above 0.4830.

xgboost · all · weather_days 5 · max_depth 3 · learning_rate 0.03 · n_estimators 1000 · subsample 0.8 · colsample_bytree 0.8 · scale_pos_weight auto · seed 553371 · 105 s

MeasuredValidation AUPRC 0.4867, up 0.0037 on the best. AUROC 0.8869. False negatives fell from 235 to 208 of 428 escapes; false positives rose from 211 to 299. Train–validation gap narrowed from 0.244 to 0.173.

DecidedKEEP as the final frozen configuration: the numeric validation winner. The gain is smaller than the aspirational 0.490 target but positive under the official selection metric, and no budget remains. The precision and calibration trade-off is threshold-dependent and does not overturn the AUPRC criterion.

Logistic regression → XGBoost same inputs, official bounded config

ObservedFull-feature logistic regression reached 0.3972 but still misses 262 of 428 validation positives, with a train–validation gap of 0.7213 versus 0.3972. The linear form, not the inputs, looks like the limit.

HypothesisKeeping the full feature protocol and five weather days fixed while replacing logistic regression with the official bounded XGBoost configuration will capture nonlinear interactions among weather, VIIRS, fuel, vegetation and location and raise validation AUPRC to at least 0.430.

DecidedKEEP. The nonlinear model extracts materially more ranking signal from the same discovery-time inputs. Train AUPRC 0.7269 against validation 0.4830 leaves a 0.2439 gap, so tree complexity is the highest-value single variable next.
